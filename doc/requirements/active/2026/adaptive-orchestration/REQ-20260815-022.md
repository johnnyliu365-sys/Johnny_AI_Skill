## Requirement-identity amendment — reviewed design, runtime not yet effective

The project owner directed on 2026-10-18 that work on an existing product capability must
revise its original PRD rather than create another PRD for each bug fix, test addition,
refactor or correction. This records the approved **design amendment** to the same
requirement identity. It does not by itself switch the currently operative revision 05
AC-17 one-to-one validator or authorize a runtime migration.

- A PRD ID is the durable identity of one product requirement. Bug fixes, test additions,
  refactors and corrections retain that identity and the approved acceptance baseline;
  they do not create a new PRD or CHG merely because an engineering ticket is opened.
- A genuine, approved change to the same product capability retains the PRD ID, advances
  its revision and records an independently identified CHG linked to the exact old and new
  revisions. Historical CHG links remain traceable; PRD and CHG are not presumed one-to-one.
- `NEW_INDEPENDENT` and `SUPERSEDE` may create a PRD only with an exact valid
  `RequirementCreationGrant`. `SUPERSEDE` also binds the replaced PRD IDs, approved
  replacement relation and retirement transition; old and replacement requirements cannot
  remain concurrently active after that transition.
- Archive and active indexes must preserve legacy matching pairs while representing the
  revised one-PRD-to-many-CHG lineage without flattening the bounded tree or losing
  historical references.

The reviewed AC-17 design distinguishes a same-requirement revision from a new
independent capability, rejects ungranted PRD creation, validates exact revisions and
approved CHG links, and proves that a correction cannot silently rewrite acceptance.
The bounded SPEC design correction is authorized and independently reviewed; until
the validator/index migration and Host grant capability are qualified and scoped
runtime activation is valid, current one-to-one AC-17 and its fail-closed tests
remain operative.

### AC-17 identity design baseline — runtime activation pending

Replace only AC-17's one-to-one requirement/change identity and retirement sentences;
keep its bounded root/partition/leaf topology and all unrelated controls unchanged:

> A PRD ID permanently identifies one product requirement. An engineering correction,
> test addition or refactor that preserves approved product behavior retains the existing
> PRD ID and revision; it neither creates a PRD nor implies a CHG. An approved change to
> that product requirement retains the PRD ID, advances its revision, and binds an
> independently identified CHG to the exact prior and new revisions, approval and
> affected acceptance conditions. One PRD may therefore have multiple historical CHGs;
> neither suffix equality nor one-to-one identity grants authority.
>
> PRD creation is denied by default. Only `NEW_INDEPENDENT` or `SUPERSEDE` with an exact
> valid `RequirementCreationGrant` may create a new PRD. `SUPERSEDE` also binds each
> replaced PRD, the approved replacement relation and the archive transition. The
> replaced and replacement requirements cannot both remain active after that transition.
> Retirement archives the PRD identity with its final effective revision, all linked
> CHG history, relevant UI/SPEC/Ticket references and replacement relation; historical
> CHG entries remain independently addressable for any other affected PRD.
>
> Active and archive indexes resolve exact PRD/revision/CHG relations as direct-child
> metadata and reject duplicates, dangling links, ambiguous revisions, unapproved
> changes, active/archive overlap and ungranted creation. Legacy matching PRD/CHG pairs
> remain readable historical sources but do not themselves authorize a new revision or
> new PRD. Until migrated validators and indexes are qualified, reject rather than
> silently reinterpret an old pair as this new model.
